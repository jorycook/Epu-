# G8 · The mascot, second time: Kaguare, the southern tamandua

Notes, 3 Nov 2026. They replace the mascot part of G5 (§0 item 2 and the Tatu'i rows), which stays as the record of the
first choice.

**Why the mascot changed.** G5 chose a tatu bolita named Tatu'i and listed "Does *tatu* have any slang or double meaning
in Paraguay?" among the questions for a native speaker. The owner answered: *tatu* is common Paraguayan vernacular for
"vagina". The sense is not only spoken: the Krivoshein de Canese and Acosta Alcaraz dictionary prints "tatu. s. Armadillo.
2. Vulva." [N1], and a Paraguayan Guarani list of insults has three entries built on it [S1]. G5's screen missed both. So
this time every candidate name had three independent screens aimed at informal use, not only at dictionaries.

**Method and limits.** Six researchers worked in parallel: the abundance question, the names, two slang screens (Paraguay;
the region and the neighbouring languages), a third adversarial re-screen, and the look and culture of the animal; a
seventh verified the facts. The network policy blocked FAUNA Paraguay, Wikipedia, Wiktionary, ABC Color, GBIF and
iNaturalist, and the shared web-search budget (200 searches per turn) ran out partway, so the re-screen and the verifier
had to rely mostly on Guarani dictionaries and corpora hosted on GitHub. Marks as in G5: **high** (two independent
sources, or a primary source), **medium** (one good source), **low** (one weak source or an inference).

---

## 0. Decisions in one screen

1. **Species: the southern tamandua** (*Tamandua tetradactyla*), the anteater the owner asked for, "whichever kind is more
   abundant in Paraguay". Confidence medium: no census or density figure exists for either species in Paraguay, but the
   tamandua is confirmed in every department and is not threatened (Least Concern, nationally and globally), while the
   giant anteater is Vulnerable and has died out across much of the Región Oriental (§1). In the Humid Chaco's palm
   savannas the giant anteater can be locally as common or more so.
2. **Name: Kaguare.** The Guarani name of the southern tamandua, written with K and no accent mark (high), the form
   Paraguayan press uses for the animal. In Spanish the animal is *el oso melero* (also spelled *oso mielero*). Labels read
   "Kaguare, el oso melero".
3. **Not Kaguare'i**, the obvious diminutive on the pattern of Tatu'i: it is the name of several localities in José
   Fassardi (Guairá), one of them tied in a YouTube video to a land dispute with a soy farmer, and it sounds like
   Portuguese *cagarei* ("I will shit"). **Not Caguaré** with a C (it reads as Spanish *cagaré*). **Never "perezoso"**:
   the dictionary's first gloss of *kaguare* is "perezoso", and the app is called ¡Levantate!.
4. **No etymology on screen.** No source gives one; "the one from the forest" and "the stinker" are both unproven.
5. **The art follows the screens:** a short, rounded snout in the colour of the face (*Tĩkaguare*, "kaguare-nose", is a
   recorded taunt); no tongue gags; nothing that makes it a pet; rounded claws; no bushy tail (that is the jurumi).
6. **A native speaker must still clear the name** before it ships: questions in §5.

| In the Atlantic Forest of Misiones, which continues into Alto Paraná and Itapúa, camera traps recorded the tamandua in 7 of 7 surveys (49 records) and the giant anteater in 3 of 7 (9 records). | high (the verifier's own tally) | ATLANTIC-CAMTRAPS 1.0 [A8] |
| Against: in the Humid Chaco's forest islets one camera-trap study had a capture success of 2.3 % for the giant anteater and 1.2 % for the tamandua; 12 giant anteaters were counted dead on the Transchaco in one day in 2007. | medium | Laino et al. 2020 [A4]; FAUNA Paraguay [A10] |

Conservation status measures the risk of decline, not headcount, and occurrence counts (GBIF, iNaturalist, blocked here)
would measure observers as much as animals. "The more abundant" rests on range, status and the eastern camera-trap
pattern. Brand copy should say "the anteater found all over Paraguay" rather than claim a number.

## 3. The slang screens

Three screens, run independently: one on Paraguayan Guarani, Jopara and Paraguayan Spanish (press, X, Facebook, YouTube
snippets, slang glossaries); one on Rioplatense and Bolivian Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese and English; and an adversarial
re-screen that assumed both had missed something. The re-screen's web searches were refused (budget spent); it used the
Guarani dictionaries, corpora and Paraguayan place lists instead.

| Candidate | Finding | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **kaguare** | Used for the animal in every press and social-media hit (rescues, sightings, wildlife TV) and in place names; no vulgar, sexual or insulting sense found [S2]. The insult list has *Tĩkaguare* (*tĩ* "nose"), most likely "kaguare-nose", a taunt for a long nose [S1]. A sound-alike of Spanish *cagaré*, undocumented as a joke. A colonial drinking root *cagua-* and modern *kagua* "cup", *kaguy* "chicha": no use of *kaguare* for a drinker found. | low, not cleared |
| **Kaguare'i** | Place names with a reported land dispute; Portuguese *cagarei*; it echoes the rejected Tatu'i. | avoid |
| **Caguaré** | Reads as Spanish *cagaré*. | avoid |
| **jurumi** | Clean, but it is the rarer species; near-homophone *jurumy'ỹi* "chismoso". | wrong species |
| **tamanduá** | Brazilian *abraço de tamanduá* (a treacherous embrace). | not a name |
| **oso melero** | Nothing found against it. | use for the animal |
| **anteater** | English slang for an uncircumcised penis (Green's Dictionary of Slang). | not used |
| **the long snout and tongue** | The snout is already an insult image (*Tĩkaguare*); a busy mouth or tongue reads as "chismoso" in Guarani; the tongue invites crude jokes. | controlled in the art |

## 4. The animal, for the art and the games

- **Look** (medium; from species accounts via snippets): golden cream to tan fur; a black "vest" over the shoulders, the
  flanks and the belly, the forelegs coming out of it like arms from armholes; round upright ears; a small dark eye; a
  pale face; strong forefeet whose big third claw opens termite mounds; a long, tapering prehensile tail, pale with dark
  blotches and naked underneath. Head and body 47 to 77 cm, tail 40 to 67 cm, 3.5 to 8.4 kg [L1]. Males and females look
  the same.
- **The stance that names the app** (high): when threatened it rears onto its hind legs, leans on its tail as a third
  support and spreads its arms [L2]. Kaguare's "Opu'ã · Se levantó" pose is that stance, made friendly: arms open, chin
  up. No source gives the Guarani or Spanish wording for it; Paraguayan Spanish says *se para*.
- **It climbs** (high: "primarily arboreal") [L3]; it walks on the outer edges of its forefeet with the claws folded in.
- **Sensitivities** (from the look and culture research): tamanduas are caged and sold illegally as pets in Paraguay
  (Última Hora, 2026), so Kaguare is never shown as a pet (no collar, no leash, no human cuddling him, no sweets or
  honey); the claws are real weapons, so they stay rounded and never slash; tamanduas die on Paraguayan roads, so no road
  jokes; no smell gags; no invented "Guarani legend" (none was found for either anteater; a Bakairi tale of the jaguar and
  the tamanduá is not Guarani).

## 5. What a Paraguayan speaker must still answer

Ask several urban Paraguayans aged 18 to 35 and a Guarani teacher, in Spanish:

1. Sin pensarlo mucho: ¿qué es lo primero que se te viene a la cabeza cuando escuchás «Kaguare»?
2. Si una app para aprender guaraní tuviera una mascota que se llama «Kaguare», ¿qué chiste o qué apodo harían tus amigos
   con ese nombre?
3. ¿«Kaguare» tiene algún significado sexual, o se usa para alguna parte del cuerpo? Contame aunque sea grosero.
4. ¿Alguna vez escuchaste que a una persona le digan «kaguare»? ¿A quién y por qué? ¿Y «tĩkaguare»? ¿Qué tan fuerte es?
5. ¿«Kaguare» se usa para decir «perezoso» o «vago»? En una app que se llama «Epu'ã» (¡Levantate!), ¿el nombre te parece
   simpático o una burla?
6. ¿«Kaguare» te suena a «cagaré»? ¿La gente se va a burlar con eso?
7. ¿Conocés una canción, un chiste de Perurimá, un meme o alguien conocido con «kaguare»?
8. ¿Decís «oso melero», «oso mielero» o «kaguare»? ¿«Oso melero» tiene doble sentido?
9. (Para un profesor) ¿Se escribe y se pronuncia *kaguare*, ka-gua-RE?
10. Control: ¿y «tatu»? ¿Tiene otro significado en Paraguay? (If the answer misses the vulgar sense, give the other
    answers less weight.)
