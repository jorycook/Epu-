| **Total** | **52** | **260** | + 52 review days, 52 week tests, 6 level exams | |

Close to Yaoki's 50 units (N5 8, N4 10, N3 12, N2 12, N1 8) [1]. B1–B2 get the most units because guided-learning hours grow by level [6]; C1–C2 get fewer, heavier units (longer texts), as Yaoki's N1 does [1].

**The day by level.** A1–A2: Yaoki's four grammar steps, with "compare" setting Spanish against Guarani (*nosotros* vs. ñande/ore), plus a 2-minute **Sonido y letra** step. B1–B2: the same, plus a weekly **mediation item** on the review day. C1–C2: the grammar slot becomes **Estilo y registro** (same four steps); the scene becomes terminology or genre; the skill item is longer.

**The level exam** (replaces the JLPT mock) has five parts: the DELE's *Comprensión de lectura, Comprensión auditiva, Expresión e interacción escritas, Expresión e interacción orales* [8], plus *Mediación*; at C1–C2 use the 2024 DELE C1 naming *Expresión, mediación e interacción* [7]. The Companion Volume (2020) adds mediation, online-interaction and plurilingual/pluricultural scales, a Pre-A1 level and a replacement phonology scale [4][5]; Pre-A1 content goes into A1 U1, not a seventh level.

## 2. Level by level

Can-do statements are my own paraphrase of [3][4], not quotations.

### A1 · Acceso: 8 units

- **Meta.** Usa y entiende fórmulas y frases muy frecuentes (saludar, presentarse, agradecer, pedir algo) si el otro habla despacio y colabora. Pregunta y responde sobre datos personales, familia y lugar. Dice cómo se siente. Lee letreros y mensajes muy breves y escribe una ficha o una nota con las letras propias del guaraní.
- **Vocabulario.** Saludos, personas, familia, casa, comida y bebida, números, días, rutinas, mercado.
- **Textos y audios.** Fichas, letreros, chats, diálogos de saludo grabados despacio, avisos de una línea.
- **Sonido y escritura.** The Academia's 33-letter alphabet (Nov 2015; rules 2016; grammar 2018): 12 vowels, half nasal; digraphs ch, mb, nd, ng, nt, rr [28][31]. y as a vowel; ã ẽ ĩ õ ũ ỹ; the puso ' as a consonant [31]. Stress and accent marks (?), from the phonology researcher. Typing ã ẽ ĩ õ ũ ỹ g̃ ' (g̃ = g + U+0303, no precomposed form).
- **Cultura.** Greetings and courtesy; tereré, on UNESCO's list since 2020 as "Prácticas y saberes tradicionales del tereré en la cultura del pohã ñana" [61]; the family table.
- **Mediación.** Pasar a castellano un dato concreto (un horario, un letrero), y al revés [4].
- **Examen A1.** Reading: signs and messages. Listening: short dialogues heard twice. Writing: a form, a chat message. Speaking: self-introduction and questions. Mediation: relay one fact from a sign.

### A2 · Plataforma: 10 units

- **Meta.** Resuelve intercambios breves y previsibles (comprar, pedir turno, preguntar el camino). Describe con frases sencillas su familia, su casa, su trabajo y lo que hizo. Entiende avisos, mensajes de voz y textos cortos y claros. Escribe notas e invitaciones.
- **Vocabulario.** Parentesco, cuerpo y salud, ciudad y transporte, compras, clima, fiestas, trabajo y estudio.
- **Textos y audios.** Menús, horarios, avisos, mensajes de voz, postales, diálogos en el mercado y en el puesto de salud, una leyenda simplificada.
- **Sonido y escritura.** Nasal harmony in affixes (nasality spreads across affixes and postpositions [33]): mbo- before oral stems, mo- before nasal ones; je-/ñe- [16][17]. Prenasalised mb, nd, ng vs. m, n, ñ; whether these alternations are allophony or allomorphy is debated [17]. Dictation of nasal words.
- **Cultura.** 25 August, Día del Idioma Guaraní: it recalls the 1967 Constitution (Guarani a national language) and has been kept since 1979 [38]. Polka and guarania (Flores, 1925; UNESCO heritage 2024) [54]. Myths such as Jasy Jatere [60].
- **Mediación.** Explicar un menú o un aviso; contarle al médico lo que dice un familiar [4].
- **Examen A2.** Reading: notices, a short narrative. Listening: announcements, voice messages. Writing: an invitation, a note. Speaking: market or health-post role-play. Mediation: explain a menu or notice.

### B1 · Umbral: 10 units

- **Meta.** Sigue lo esencial de conversaciones, noticias breves y textos claros sobre temas conocidos. Cuenta experiencias, planes y opiniones con razones simples. Se arregla en un viaje o una estadía en el interior. Resume para otra persona lo que leyó u oyó.
- **Vocabulario.** Vida rural y urbana, trabajo, relaciones, costumbres, fiestas patronales, medio ambiente, tecnología cotidiana.
- **Textos y audios.** Short news items from the state agency IP's Guarani section [42]; letters, blogs, instructions, short interviews; oral tales, simple lyrics.
- **Cultura.** The seven sons of Tau and Kerana, as one source lists them: Teju Jagua, Jasy Jatere, Moñai, Mbo'i tui, Kurupi, Ao Ao, Luisón (order and spelling vary between sources) [60]; Perurimá, the Paraguayan Pedro Urdemales [59]; tereré and pohã ñana [61].
- **Mediación.** Resumir una noticia en castellano; tomar notas de un audio; intermediario informal en el mercado [4].
- **Examen B1.** Reading: news, a letter. Listening: interview, announcements. Writing: request letter. Speaking: story and opinion. Mediation: spoken summary across languages.

### B2 · Avanzado: 10 units

- **Meta.** Entiende las ideas principales de textos complejos y de programas de radio y TV en guaraní estándar y en jopara. Argumenta a favor y en contra. Conversa con soltura sin forzar al interlocutor. Distingue lo visto, lo oído y lo descubierto (evidencialidad). Escribe textos claros y detallados.
- **Vocabulario.** Sociedad y política, educación bilingüe, historia, medios, campo y producción, salud pública.
- **Textos y audios.** Opinion pieces, reports, radio programmes, TV talk shows (Paraguay TV's *Ñemongeta*, in Guarani and Jopara [42]), short stories, polka lyrics.
- **Cultura.** Bilingualism and diglossia; Ley 1264/1998 has the second official language taught from the start of school [41]; *Cabichuí* (1867–1868), a bilingual war weekly [57]; the Chaco War in Emiliano R. Fernández's songs [47].
- **Mediación.** Traducir un aviso o una carta; explicar las referencias de una canción; facilitar una conversación con desacuerdo leve [4].
- **Examen B2.** Reading: opinion piece. Listening: radio segment. Writing: argumentative essay. Speaking: debate. Mediation: translate a notice, explain a song.

### C1 · Dominio operativo eficaz: 8 units

- **Meta.** Entiende textos largos y exigentes (leyes, informes, noticieros rápidos) y percibe registro e intención. Elige entre jopara y guaraní estándar según la situación. Redacta notas, informes y discursos bien organizados. Media entre castellano y guaraní en salud, justicia y administración.
- **Estilo y registro.** The Academy's grammar (2018) warns against Spanish syntactic calques [29]. Guaraniete is "uninterfered" Guarani; some studies doubt non-Indigenous people speak it daily [46].
- **Textos y audios.** Ley 4251, bilingual SPL edition (*Léi 4251/10 Ñe'ẽnguéra Rehegua*) [35]; the judiciary's *Diccionario General Bilingüe* (2023) [39]; IP news, *Ñemongeta* [42]; speeches; popular science.
- **Cultura (the State's Guarani).**
  - Constitution 1992, art. 140 ("país pluricultural y bilingüe"); a transitional article orders an official Guarani edition, the Spanish text prevailing in doubt [37].
  - Ley 4251/2010 regulates arts. 77 and 140. Art. 3: official languages in the three branches. Art. 14: laws and official publicity in both. Art. 17: both languages for public posts. Arts. 31–34: the SPL. Arts. 43–46: the Academia (norms for orthography, lexicon, terminology, grammar) [35]. Numbers from search snippets: check the PDF. Art. 51 (written duties three years after alphabet and grammar) is from a blog only [36].
  - Judiciary: language office since 2012; official Guarani since Acordada 838/13; a 40-hour *Curso Básico de Guaraní Jurídico* [39].
- **Mediación.** Resumir un informe para una asamblea; interpretar en salud o justicia; simplificar un texto para hablantes de jopara [4].
- **Examen C1.** Reading: legal or administrative text. Listening: fast radio bulletin. Writing: formal note and summary. Speaking: short speech and interpreted dialogue [7].

### C2 · Maestría: 6 units

- **Meta.** Comprende prácticamente todo, incluidas la literatura, la canción, la ironía y el humor. Reconstruye relatos y argumentos de fuentes diversas. Matiza con precisión, juega con la lengua y traduce textos literarios explicando lo intraducible. Reconoce otras variedades guaraníes y actúa con respeto en espacios pluriculturales.
- **Textos y audios.** Poems, plays, novels (excerpts only where licensing allows; else summary and analysis), lyrics, oral tales, ñe'ẽnga, other varieties (recordings only with community consent).
- **Mediación.** Traducción literaria comentada; explicar humor e ironía; facilitar un espacio pluricultural entre variedades y pueblos [4].
- **Examen C2.** Reading: literary text and analysis. Listening: oral tale or debate. Writing: commented translation. Speaking: present a work. Mediation: explain a ñe'ẽnga or a joke.

## 5. Why this distribution, and how published Guarani courses compare

1. **Published Guarani sequences stop at an intermediate level.** *Hablemos el guaraní* (CEPAG; Diego Ortiz S.J. with D. Aguilera and E. Marecos, from 1984) has four levels for foreigners [70]. UFF has four (Iniciante I–II, Intermediário I–II, since 2017) [66]. The Ateneo's *Curso Básico* has trained over 140,000 people [71]. Also: FHCE Uruguay's four-month basic course [69], UNTREF's Nivel I [68], the judiciary's 40-hour course [39], Duolingo's Jopara-based course (by 2016) [73], and the Peace Corps course, which starts with pronunciation and greetings [72]. The only CEFR-aligned work found covers A1–A2: a UNI/CONACYT A1 project and an AILA 2023 abstract [74]. So the C levels here are built from domains that exist: the State's Guarani, the courts, the media, terminology and literature.
2. **Sound and writing first.** UNI's *Guaraní I* goes from sound structure to writing (syllables, diphthongs, digraphs); *Guaraní II* adds grammar and Natalicio Talavera and anonymous wartime songs [67]. Hence a sound-heavy A1 U1 and literature that enters gradually.
3. **Spanish speakers have the vocabulary, not the morphology.** Jopara keeps Guarani morphology while borrowing Spanish words [46][24]. Learners recognise roots early but meet an active–stative, polysynthetic system [10]. So A1–A2 carry the core morphology and the Spanish/Guarani "compare" step.
4. **Hours grow by level** (Cambridge: A1 c. 90–100 hours up to C2 c. 1,000–1,200; sources disagree on whether these are cumulative) [6]. So B1–B2 get the most units, and the C levels get longer days rather than more grammar.
5. **CEFR levels are can-do based and may be subdivided** [2]; the Instituto Cervantes curriculum turns levels into inventories (grammar, pronunciation, functions, genres, cultural references, intercultural skills) [9]. The level headers follow that model.

## Recommendations for the app

1. **Adopt 8/10/10/10/8/6 = 52 units**, with one level menu, one plan that can start at any level after a placement test, and one notebook across levels [1]. Sound is stored per unit, as in Yaoki.
2. **Keep the Yaoki day** (two grammar points; teach, compare, drill, exam-style), plus *Sonido y letra* at A1–A2, a weekly mediation item from B1, and *Estilo y registro* in the grammar slot at C1–C2.
3. **One five-part exam per level** (the four DELE skills plus *Mediación*; DELE C1 2024 naming at the C levels [7]), plus a mid-level mini exam like Yaoki's mini mocks. Receptive parts are auto-scored; productive parts use a rubric, can-do self-assessment and optional teacher review.
4. **Tag register on every item** (`registro: jopara | estándar | guaraniete`). Teach Jopara as what people say and the Academy standard as the written norm: *celu* (said) vs. *pumbyry* (standard) [43]; Spanish figures (said) vs. Academy numerals (once confirmed).
5. **Add fields** `cefr`, `skill`, `registro`, `fuente` (sourced or composed), `confianza`, `revisión_nativa` to the day JSON, and make the build refuse composed Guarani that has no native review.
6. **Indigenous varieties: awareness only.** No recordings without community consent; *Ayvu Rapyta* is discussed, never drilled [55].
7. **C1 corpora**: Ley 4251 (bilingual), the Constitution's Guarani edition, the judiciary's dictionary [35][37][39], after a reuse check by the licensing researcher.
8. **Literature**: public-domain texts only after the §3 check; otherwise summary, analysis and short quotation, with recordings linked rather than bundled [75].

## Open questions / needs a native speaker

2. Every form marked (?) must be confirmed in [10] or [11] before content is written. They include -rõ/*ramo*, -vo, -jave, -rire, -mboyve, -ve/-gui, -vete, -icha, -eterei, -nte, *avei*, -voi, *katu*, -'ỹ, -ha/-hára/-pyre/*tembi-*, -jey, -pa, -pota, -ne, -jepi, -tava.
